Budgeting from payday to payday

If you're not paid on the 1st, start your budget month on payday – for example from the 15th to the 14th of the next month. Then every pay cheque belongs to exactly one budget month. If you're paid on the 28th or later, the alternative is often simpler: treat it as next month's money and start on the 1st anyway.

The trouble with calendar months

If you're paid on the 15th, half the money is gone by the 1st – yet you count it as a fresh month. The numbers never add up: in the first half there seems to be too much, in the second too little.

Option 1: budget month starts on payday

  1. Pick the day your pay usually arrives, for example the 15th.
  2. Assign fixed costs to that period. Rent due on the 1st then falls into the budget month that started on the 15th of the previous month.
  3. Use the real number of days for your daily budget. 15 January to 14 February is 31 days; 15 February to 14 March is 28 or 29.

If the money arrives a day early or late now and then, the plan doesn't change. What matters is a fixed start day, not the exact arrival.

Paid on the 29th, 30th or 31st?

Not every month has those days. Then the last day of the month is the sensible choice: paid on the 31st, your February starts on the 28th (or 29th), your April on the 30th.

Option 2: money from the 28th is next month's money

If your pay arrives just before the end of the month, many people still start the budget month on the 1st. The money from the 28th isn't touched – it's the budget for the coming month.

The second option needs a one-off buffer: the first time, pay from the 28th has to last until the end of the following month.

With the app

Choose when your budget starts

In Simple Household Budget, Settings → Budget starts takes any day from the 1st to the 31st. For the 29th to the 31st, the app uses the last day in shorter months. The change applies from the next period, so the current one isn't thrown off.

Income has its own date (“Arrives on”). The app counts pay only from the day it arrives – before that, the large number can be lower or even negative. The bar doesn't turn red because of that; red only appears when you really spend more than the month allows.

Questions

What about fixed costs that leave on the 1st?

They belong to the budget month that contains the 1st. If your period starts on the 15th, rent on the 1st is an expense of the period that began on the 15th of the previous month – you pay it from the pay cheque before.

I'm paid twice a month. What then?

Use the day of the larger payment as the start, and plan the second as additional income in the same period.

How do I switch without counting a month twice?

Switch at the next payday: the current period ends the day before, and the new one starts on payday. A one-off shorter or longer transition period is normal.

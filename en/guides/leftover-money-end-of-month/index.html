Money left at the end of the month – or not enough?

If there's money left at the end of the month, you can roll it into next month, save it, or start the new month from scratch. A shortfall, on the other hand, should almost always be carried over – otherwise your budget counts money that doesn't exist.

When there's something left: three options

OptionWhat happensGood when …
Roll overThe rest increases next month's budgetexpensive months (holidays, gifts) are coming
SaveThe rest goes to savings, the new month starts as usualyou have a savings goal
Start freshEvery month starts with the same amountyou want to look at each month on its own

Rolling over is easy but has a catch: a big cushion invites spending. If you want to save, transfer the rest deliberately and start fresh.

When it wasn't enough

A shortfall at month's end usually means your overdraft or savings stepped in. If the new month still starts with a full budget, the missing money stays invisible.

  1. Carry the shortfall over: the deficit is taken off next month. Unpleasant, but honest.
  2. Find the cause: was it a one-off (repair, gift) or does it happen every month? A look through your history will tell.
  3. If it keeps happening, check your fixed costs – that's usually where the biggest savings are. The fixed expenses list helps.

Better: notice before the month ends

You don't have to wait until the 30th to see whether it will last. Mid-month, compare how much of your money is spent with how much of the month has passed. Here's how: How much can I spend per day?

With the app

Surplus and shortfall, set separately

Simple Household Budget has two switches in the settings: Carry surplus over – what's left moves to the next period – and Carry shortfall over – a deficit is deducted next period. Turn on both, one or neither. Changes apply from the next period.

The app shows red only for a real overspend, when you've spent more than the month allows. If you're just ahead of the month, the bar beyond the time mark changes colour as an early warning.

Questions

Is it bad to overspend in one month?

A single month with a repair or birthdays is normal. What matters is carrying the shortfall over or covering it deliberately from savings – and seeing whether it was a one-off.

Should I save the rest or roll it over?

If you have a savings goal: save. If expensive months are coming: roll it over. Either is better than quietly spending it next month.

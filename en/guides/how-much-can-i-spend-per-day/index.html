How much can I spend per day?

Take your take-home pay, subtract fixed costs and savings, and divide the rest by the days in the month. With €2,100 income, €1,150 fixed costs and €100 savings, €850 is left – a little over €28 a day in a 30-day month. Mid-month, divide what's still left by the days that remain.

Your daily budget in three steps

  1. Know your income. What arrives in your account each month after tax: wages or salary, plus regular income such as child benefit or maintenance.
  2. Subtract fixed costs and savings. Everything that leaves every month for sure: rent, utilities, insurance, subscriptions – and the amount you want to save. A full checklist: fixed expenses list.
  3. Divide by the days. Divide the rest by the number of days in the month (28 to 31).

Daily budget = (income − fixed costs − savings) ÷ days in the month

Example with made-up numbers
Take-home pay€2,100.00
− Fixed costs€1,150.00
− Savings€100.00
= Free for the month€850.00
÷ 30 days
Daily budget€28.33

That €850 is your money for everything that varies: groceries, toiletries, eating out, clothes, gifts, small repairs.

Mid-month: what's left divided by the days left

At the start of the month the sum is easy. The more interesting question comes on the 18th: will it last? Then you work with what is still there and the days still to come – today included.

Daily budget from today = still available ÷ days remaining

Example: a 30-day month
Still available on the 18th€410.00
Days remaining (18th to 30th)13
Per day from today€31.54

If the new figure is above your original daily budget, you're doing fine. If it's below, you know now – not on the 29th.

Why a daily budget isn't a daily limit

Hardly anyone spends the same every day. A weekly shop costs €60, then three days go by with no spending at all. Your daily budget is an average, not a ban. Treat it as a hard limit and shopping day feels like failure – and that's when people give up.

A better check is your pace: are you spending faster than the month is passing?

The pace check: percent spent vs. percent of the month

Compare two percentages:

  • How much of the month has passed? On the 12th of a 30-day month, 40%.
  • How much of your free money is spent? €340 of €850 is 40%.
Spent compared with the monthMeaning
less than has passedYou're on track, with room to spare.
about the sameRight on pace – it will work out.
clearly more than has passedToo fast. A few quiet days will even it out.

This check forgives the big weekly shop as long as the rest of the week stays quiet – and still warns you early enough.

Three things that throw the numbers off

  • Forgetting yearly bills. Car insurance or liability cover come once a year and leave a hole. How to plan for them: yearly bills in a monthly budget.
  • Pay doesn't arrive on the 1st. Then the calendar month doesn't match your money. The fix: budget from payday to payday.
  • Tracking in your head. Without writing it down, almost everyone guesses too low. The amount alone is enough – no category needed.

Questions

Should I subtract savings first?

Yes. Treat what you want to save like a fixed cost and take it off at the start of the month. Anything left at the end is then a bonus – not your only source of savings.

Do groceries count as fixed costs?

No. Groceries vary from week to week and belong in your daily budget. Fixed costs are amounts that leave every month at the same or nearly the same size.

What is a realistic daily budget?

That depends entirely on your income and fixed costs – there's no general figure. More important than the size is that you know it and check it again mid-month.

How do I calculate a 31-day month?

The same way, just divide by 31. With €850 that's €27.42 instead of €28.33 a day. In February it's more per day, because the same amount is spread over fewer days.

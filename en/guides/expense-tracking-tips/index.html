How to track expenses and stick with it: 7 tips

A budget rarely fails because of maths – it fails because of effort. The less you have to do per expense, the longer you'll keep going: record only the amount, and do it right away. Enter fixed costs once. Instead of many charts, watch one number – what's left.

Seven tips

  1. Record only the amount. Category, shop and note are nice but not necessary. The amount alone answers the key question: how much is left?
  2. Enter it right away, not in the evening. At the till it takes seconds. By evening you've forgotten half, by Sunday everything.
  3. Enter fixed costs once, not every month. Rent and insurance rarely change. Noted properly once, they deduct themselves.
  4. Add forgotten ones later instead of giving up. One missing entry is no reason to abandon the whole budget. Add it with its date when you remember.
  5. Watch one number. What's left after fixed costs and spending – that's all you need day to day. Analysis is for the end of the month.
  6. Use your receipts. If you'd rather not type, keep receipts or photograph them and enter the total.
  7. Look back once a month. Five minutes: what was unusual? Where did more go than you thought? That's where the real value comes from.

Why budgets fail

  • Too many required fields. Category, account, date, note – after a week every coffee feels like bookkeeping.
  • Perfectionism. One forgotten amount and everything seems wrong. Yet a few euros barely change the picture.
  • Too many categories. With twenty categories you hesitate at every purchase. A handful is enough – or none at all.
  • No answer in daily life. A spreadsheet you only open at month's end doesn't help in the supermarket. A number you can see any time does.

Questions

Do I really have to write down every little thing?

At first, yes – the little things are what surprise people. After a few months you know where your money goes, and a forgotten coffee won't change the picture.

How many categories make sense?

As few as possible. Groceries, eating out, transport, leisure and a handful more are enough for an overview. If you'd rather not pick a category, skip it.

What about cash?

Record the expense when you pay – not the cash withdrawal at the machine. Otherwise the money counts twice or not at all.
